The Problem

Know When Members Leave. You Don’t Know Why.

An expiration date passes. A “we miss you” email goes out. Maybe someone picks up the phone. But nobody knows why the member left, so every outreach is a guess.

Why did they leave?

  • Cost too high?
  • Not relevant anymore?
  • Career change?
  • Just forgot?

Some left on purpose. Some just lost track. Some would come back if anyone made it easy.

The association can’t tell the difference, because no one ever asked.

72%of the 54 members who answered said they didn’t know their membership had expired.One medical society

Many lapsed members don’t even know they’ve lapsed. That makes “Did you know your membership expired?” a better first question than a request to rejoin.

The first reason a member gives isn’t always the whole story. When someone says they’re retiring or leaving the field, a follow-up question can check whether cost is part of it, so the offer matches the real reason.

Two Tracks

A Reengagement Campaign for Every Lapse Window

PropFuel runs a separate reengagement campaign for each lapse window. The recently lapsed track starts automatically when an expiration date passes without renewal, so members who lapse after your renewal sequence move straight into it.

Recently Lapsed

0–6 months past expiration

  1. Acknowledge the Lapse

    Warm, personal outreach that says “we noticed.”

  2. Ask Why

    Surface the reason: cost, relevance, career change, or just forgot.

  3. Branch by Answer

    Cost concerns get a payment plan. Relevance issues get a value pitch.

  4. Close or Escalate

    Auto-renewal link or staff alert for personal follow-up.

Example Questions: Recently Lapsed

  • “We noticed you haven’t renewed. What was the biggest reason?”
  • “If we could change one thing about membership, what would it be?”
  • “Would a multi-year or payment plan option make a difference?”

Dormant Members

6+ months to years past expiration

  1. Re-Introduce Yourself

    They may not remember you. Lead with value, not payment.

  2. Test Interest

    Ask what has changed in their career. Find the new hook.

  3. Make It Easy

    One-click rejoin link tailored to their response.

Example Questions: Dormant

  • “It’s been a while. What are you focused on in your career right now?”
  • “Which of these would be most useful to you this year?”
  • “Would you like to see what’s new since you were last a member?”

FAQ

Questions About Win-Back Campaigns

How soon after lapse should we reach out?

Immediately, within the first 30 days. The longer you wait, the harder it gets. PropFuel’s recently lapsed track starts sending reengagement emails automatically when a member’s expiration passes without renewal.

What about members who lapsed years ago?

PropFuel’s dormant member track uses a different approach: re-introduction rather than reminder. Its reengagement emails acknowledge the time gap and lead with value rather than payment.

How do we handle members who say they left intentionally?

That response is captured and categorized. You learn the reason, the data feeds your retention strategy, and the member is removed from further outreach. Not every lapsed member should be won back, but you should always know why they left.

Can we offer discounts or payment plans through PropFuel?

Yes. PropFuel can branch based on the stated reason for lapsing. If a member says cost is the issue, the follow-up can include a payment plan link or discount code. If they say irrelevance, they get a different value-focused message.

What win-back rates are realistic?

Results depend on your list and how long members have been gone. In PropFuel case studies, a medical society saw a 23% engagement rate on its lapsed list and AWHONN saw 18%, and 88% of AWHONN’s lapsed members who answered said yes to rejoining. Even members who do not rejoin provide data that improves your retention strategy.
